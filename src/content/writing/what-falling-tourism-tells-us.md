[Semafor's report on declining US tourism](https://www.semafor.com/article/09/29/2026/decline-in-tourism-hits-us-economy) caught my attention because travel decisions connect public policy to everyday spending.

The September 29 article reports a decline in visitors and discusses possible causes, including strained relationships with countries whose residents visit the US. It also points to competing destinations.

I find that interesting because tourism depends on more than whether a place has attractions. Cost, entry requirements, exchange rates, and how welcome people expect to feel can all enter the decision.

The reported decline and the explanation for it are separate questions. An article describing both does not establish how much each factor caused the change.

Before drawing a current conclusion, I would want updated visitor and spending data. I would also separate the effects on hotels, restaurants, airlines, and the places that depend most on international visitors.
