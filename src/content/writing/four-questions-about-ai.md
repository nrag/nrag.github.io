These four links from Daron Acemoglu made me think about how we choose what AI should do. I read them as prompts for questions, rather than settled forecasts.

[Intelligence versus distorted intelligence](https://x.com/DAcemogluMIT/status/2104957957452779860): I find the distinction interesting because capability alone does not tell us whether a system helps people make better decisions. What outcome should count as useful intelligence?

[Democracy, technocracy, and who decides](https://x.com/DAcemogluMIT/status/2105348579187699989): this interests me because technical choices can affect people who have no say in making them. How should those people participate?

[The AI boom, crash risk, and inequality](https://x.com/DAcemogluMIT/status/2105760557546574239): I want to separate investment expectations from who receives the benefits. What evidence would change our view of either?

[AI investment and public health](https://x.com/DAcemogluMIT/status/2106133404806676644): I find this question useful because money and attention have competing uses. How should we compare the outcomes those investments produce?

These are my questions prompted by the links. Acemoglu's arguments and forecasts need to be read on their own terms.
